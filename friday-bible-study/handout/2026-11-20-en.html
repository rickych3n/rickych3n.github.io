11/20 Bible Study | Knowing Jesus' Authority — Words That Come from the Heart

Matthew 12:22–37 | Leader: Xiaoqi

Scripture | Matthew 12:22–37 (WEB)

22 Then one possessed by a demon, blind and mute, was brought to him; and he healed him, so that the blind and mute man both spoke and saw.

23 All the multitudes were amazed, and said, “Can this be the son of David?”

24 But when the Pharisees heard it, they said, “This man does not cast out demons except by Beelzebul, the prince of the demons.”

25 Knowing their thoughts, Jesus said to them, “Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation, and every city or house divided against itself will not stand.

26 If Satan casts out Satan, he is divided against himself. How then will his kingdom stand?

27 If I by Beelzebul cast out demons, by whom do your children cast them out? Therefore they will be your judges.

28 But if I by the Spirit of God cast out demons, then God’s Kingdom has come upon you.

29 Or how can one enter into the house of the strong man and plunder his goods, unless he first bind the strong man? Then he will plunder his house.

30 “He who is not with me is against me, and he who doesn’t gather with me, scatters.

31 Therefore I tell you, every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men, but the blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven men.

32 Whoever speaks a word against the Son of Man, it will be forgiven him; but whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him, either in this age, or in that which is to come.

33 “Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree corrupt and its fruit corrupt; for the tree is known by its fruit.

34 You offspring of vipers, how can you, being evil, speak good things? For out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks.

35 The good man out of his good treasure brings out good things, and the evil man out of his evil treasure brings out evil things.

36 I tell you that every idle word that men speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment.

37 For by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.”

Introduction

Have you noticed that different people can interpret the same event in completely different ways? Some see another person’s good deed and thank God; others suspect the motive. Why do we judge the same facts so differently? It has to do not only with what our eyes see, but with what our hearts believe.

In Matthew 12:22–37, Jesus heals a man possessed by a demon, blind and mute. The crowds are amazed and begin to wonder whether Jesus is the Son of David; the Pharisees, however, accuse Him of casting out demons by the prince of demons. Through this, Jesus teaches about the coming of God’s Kingdom, the gravity of blasphemy against the Holy Spirit, and how our words reveal our hearts.

Today we will reflect: How do I know Jesus? Am I willing to receive the truth? And what do my words reveal about my heart?

🔗 Chain-Reference Study, Verse by Verse

Verses 22–23: “Then one possessed by a demon, blind and mute, was brought to him... All the multitudes were amazed, and said, ‘Can this be the son of David?’”

Context BeadMatthew 12:14, 20–21“But the Pharisees went out, and conspired against him, how they might destroy him.” ... “He won’t break a bruised reed. He won’t quench a smoking flax, until he leads justice to victory. In his name, the nations will hope.”

The Servant of the previous passage has only just been introduced, and here we see His actual work: a demon-possessed, blind and mute man is brought before Him. The same act draws two completely different responses — the crowds’ amazed question, and the Pharisees’ accusation (v24).

Core Bead 1Isaiah 35:5–6“Then the eyes of the blind will be opened, and the ears of the deaf will be unstopped. Then the lame man will leap like a deer, and the tongue of the mute will sing.”

Isaiah foretold the day God comes to save, and its marks are exactly these — “the blind see, the mute speak” — and this man shows both at once. The crowds’ amazement is not baseless: they recognized the picture from the prophet’s book.

Core Bead 22 Samuel 7:16“Your house and your kingdom will be made sure forever before you. Your throne will be established forever.”

God promised David that his descendant would sit on the throne forever. The crowds’ “son of David” connects Jesus to this line of promise — what they are really asking is: is He the promised One?

Side BeadMatthew 9:27, 32–34Two blind men cried out, “Have mercy on us, you son of David!” ... When the demon was cast out, the mute man spoke. But the Pharisees said, “By the prince of the demons, he casts out demons.”

The same title, the same healing, the same accusation — Matthew already recorded it once in chapter 9. Chapter 12 is not a new conflict; it is the same division surfacing a second time, and this time Jesus answers it directly.

Verse 24: “This man does not cast out demons except by Beelzebul, the prince of the demons”

Core Bead2 Kings 1:2–3King Ahaziah of Israel sent messengers to inquire of Baal Zebub, the god of Ekron, and Elijah stopped the messengers with the word of Yahweh: “Is it because there is no God in Israel, that you go to inquire of Baal Zebub, the god of Ekron?”

“Beelzebul” (Baal Zebub) was originally the name of the idol of Ekron, a Philistine city — in the Old Testament, going to inquire of Baal Zebub was precisely what it meant to act as though “there is no God in Israel.” The Pharisees take this idol’s name and pin it on Jesus’ work.

Side BeadMatthew 10:25“It is enough for the disciple that he be like his teacher, and the servant like his lord. If they have called the master of the house Beelzebul, how much more those of his household!”

When Jesus sent out the twelve, He had already said it: the Master of the house (meaning Himself) would be called Beelzebul. The Pharisees said it once in chapter 9 (9:34); here is the second time — Jesus is not surprised by this accusation.

Verses 25–27: “Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation... by whom do your children cast them out?”

Core Bead1 Chronicles 28:9“Yahweh searches all hearts, and understands all the imaginations of the thoughts.”

The Pharisees’ accusation was spoken aloud, but Jesus answers what they never said — their “thoughts.” David’s dying words to Solomon find their first fulfillment here in Jesus; and the second half of this passage is also about speech (vv34–37), which likewise begins from the “heart.”

Core Bead 2Acts 19:13–16The seven sons of Sceva, Jewish exorcists, presumptuously invoked the name of the Lord Jesus to cast out demons; the evil spirit answered, “Jesus I know, and Paul I know, but who are you?” And the man in whom the evil spirit was leaped on them and overpowered them.

There really were Jews who cast out demons, and the Pharisees never said they did it by Beelzebul. Jesus’ counter-question (v27) points to exactly this double standard — the same act, judged the opposite way when the person changes.

Side BeadMark 3:23–26“How can Satan cast out Satan? If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.”

Mark records the same answer, shorter and more direct. All three Synoptic Gospels preserve this passage, showing how much weight it carried in the early church’s memory.

Verse 28: “But if I by the Spirit of God cast out demons, then God’s Kingdom has come upon you”

Core BeadActs 10:38“how God anointed him with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with him.”

When Peter later preached Jesus to Cornelius’ household, he used exactly the framework of v28: anointing by the Holy Spirit plus freeing those oppressed by the devil. The man of v22, “possessed by a demon,” is one of those “oppressed by the devil.”

Core Bead 2Exodus 8:18–19The magicians of Egypt said to Pharaoh, “This is God’s finger.”

Luke’s parallel (Luke 11:20) says “by the finger of God” where Matthew says “by the Spirit of God” — in Exodus, even Egypt’s magicians admitted defeat before God’s finger; here, the Pharisees see the same kind of work and call it Beelzebul. Read Matthew and Luke together, and the contrast stands out.

Side BeadDaniel 7:13–14“behold, one like a son of man came with the clouds of heaven... There was given him dominion, glory, and a kingdom, that all the peoples, nations, and languages should serve him. His dominion is an everlasting dominion, which will not pass away, and his kingdom that which will not be destroyed.”

In Daniel’s night vision, that never-ending kingdom — Jesus says it does not only begin in the future; it “has come upon you” already, in His works of casting out demons and healing.

Verses 29–30: “Unless he first bind the strong man, then he will plunder his house... He who is not with me is against me”

Core BeadIsaiah 49:24–25“Shall the prey be taken from the mighty, or the lawful captives be delivered? But Yahweh says, ‘Even the captives of the mighty shall be taken away, and the prey of the terrible shall be delivered.’”

Isaiah had already used this same picture: the captives in the strong man’s hands will be taken back. Jesus entering the strong man’s house to plunder his goods — the demon-possessed man is the one taken back — is this promise happening on the spot.

Side BeadMark 9:40“For whoever is not against us is on our side.”

Jesus said this to His disciples when they saw someone casting out demons in His name and wanted to stop him: whoever is not against us is for us. The same boundary, stated from the other side in v30: whoever is not with Him is against Him. Put together, the line is drawn at “one’s attitude toward Jesus,” not at “whether one is one of us.”

Verses 31–32: “But the blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven”

Core BeadIsaiah 63:10“But they rebelled, and grieved his Holy Spirit. Therefore he turned and became their enemy, and himself fought against them.”

The generation of the Exodus saw God’s works with their own eyes in the wilderness, yet rebelled until they grieved the Holy Spirit. Isaiah’s words looking back on that history meet the Pharisees’ situation exactly: they have seen, and still refuse.

Core Bead 2Hebrews 10:29“How much worse punishment do you think he will be judged worthy of who has trodden under foot the Son of God, and has counted the blood of the covenant with which he was sanctified an unholy thing, and has insulted the Spirit of grace?”

Hebrews uses the phrase “insulted the Spirit of grace” — the closest phrase in the New Testament to “blasphemy against the Holy Spirit.” Its warning is aimed at the same people: those who have come to know the truth and yet deliberately treat it lightly.

Side BeadLuke 12:10“Everyone who speaks a word against the Son of Man will be forgiven, but those who blaspheme against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven.”

Luke places this saying in the teaching on confessing Christ before men (Luke 12:8–10) — the same warning: in Matthew it answers an accusation, in Luke it encourages the disciples to confess Him openly.

Verses 33–35: “By their fruits you will know them... For out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks”

Core BeadMatthew 7:16–20“By their fruits you will know them. Do you gather grapes from thorns, or figs from thistles? Even so, every good tree produces good fruit, but the corrupt tree produces evil fruit.”

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus had already taught “know them by their fruit” — then it was about telling false prophets apart; here He turns the same principle on the Pharisees themselves — the accusation of v24 is the fruit their own tree bears.

Core Bead 2Luke 6:45“The good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings out that which is good, and the evil man out of the evil treasure of his heart brings out that which is evil, for out of the abundance of the heart, his mouth speaks.”

Luke’s parallel account is almost word-for-word the same as vv34–35. Matthew and Luke both place this saying right after “tree and fruit” — speech is not a surface matter; it is the outlet for what the heart has stored.

Side BeadMatthew 3:7“You offspring of vipers, who warned you to flee from the wrath to come?”

Side BeadProverbs 4:23“Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring of life.”

John the Baptist called the Pharisees “offspring of vipers” once before at the Jordan (Matthew 3:7); Jesus uses the same address for the same group here. Proverbs states the principle in full: life’s outcomes flow from the heart — so what must be guarded is the heart.

Verse 36: “Every idle word that men speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment”

Core BeadProverbs 18:21“Death and life are in the power of the tongue; those who love it will eat its fruit.”

Proverbs already put the weight of the tongue in the strongest terms — life and death. Jesus’ “idle words” are words spoken casually, seemingly of no consequence; that even such words must bear fruit and be accounted for word by word is precisely because words are never neutral.

Side BeadJames 3:9–10“With it we bless our God and Father, and with it we curse men, who are made in the image of God. Out of the same mouth comes blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to be so!”

James applies the principle of v34 to life in the church: the same mouth that blesses on Sunday curses on weekdays — he is asking the question of v35: what, after all, is stored in your heart?

Verse 37: “For by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned”

Core BeadRomans 10:9–10“that if you will confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart, one believes resulting in righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made resulting in salvation.”

Paul places “heart” and “mouth” side by side: believe in the heart, confess with the mouth — the positive version of v34, “out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks.” The passage began with the Pharisees’ words (v24) and closes here: words are evidence, showing which side a heart stands on.

Closing BeadMatthew 15:18–19“But the things which proceed out of the mouth come out of the heart, and they defile the man. For out of the heart come evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, sexual sins, thefts, false testimony, and blasphemies.”

After chapter 3, Jesus teaches the same principle a second time to the same opponents — in Matthew, “heart and mouth” is a theme He returns to again and again; this passage in Matthew 12 is its fullest statement.

The Whole Chain in One Sentence: The crowds asked, “Can this be the son of David?” (2 Samuel 7:16), because the blind seeing and the mute speaking are exactly the picture Isaiah foretold (Isaiah 35:5–6); but the Pharisees attributed His work to Baal Zebub, the idol of Ekron (2 Kings 1:3). Jesus showed that a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand, and declared that casting out demons by the Spirit of God means the Kingdom of God has come (Exodus 8:19; Acts 10:38); He binds the strong man and takes back the captives (Isaiah 49:24–25), and warns against insulting the Spirit’s witness (Isaiah 63:10; Hebrews 10:29); and He returns at last to His constant teaching — a tree is known by its fruit (Matthew 7:16), the mouth speaks what the heart is full of (Luke 6:45), and even idle words must be accounted for (Proverbs 18:21), because words reveal the heart (Romans 10:9–10).

Study Questions

Question 1

Scripture (Matthew 12:22–23): “Then one possessed by a demon, blind and mute, was brought to him; and he healed him, so that the blind and mute man both spoke and saw. All the multitudes were amazed, and said, ‘Can this be the son of David?’”

Question: What kind of person did Jesus heal? How did the crowds respond?

Answer: The man was possessed by a demon, blind and mute; Jesus healed him so that he could both speak and see. The crowds were amazed and asked whether He was “the son of David” — the long-awaited Messiah.

Question 2

Scripture (Matthew 12:24): “But when the Pharisees heard it, they said, ‘This man does not cast out demons except by Beelzebul, the prince of the demons.’”

Question: How did the Pharisees explain Jesus’ casting out of demons? What attitude does this show?

Answer: They said Jesus cast out demons by Beelzebul, the prince of demons. They did not deny the miracle, yet they attributed Jesus’ work to an evil power — an attitude of hostility toward Him.

Question 3

Scripture (Matthew 12:25–27): “Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation, and every city or house divided against itself will not stand. If Satan casts out Satan, he is divided against himself. How then will his kingdom stand? If I by Beelzebul cast out demons, by whom do your children cast them out? Therefore they will be your judges.”

Question: How did Jesus refute the Pharisees’ accusation?

Answer: A kingdom, city, or household divided against itself cannot stand; if Satan casts out Satan, he is destroying his own kingdom. Jesus also pointed to their own children who cast out demons, showing that their standard of judgment was inconsistent.

Question 4

Scripture (Matthew 12:28): “But if I by the Spirit of God cast out demons, then God’s Kingdom has come upon you.”

Question: What does “the Kingdom of God has come upon you” mean?

Answer: Jesus cast out demons by the Spirit of God, showing that God’s reign and salvation had come through His ministry, and that the powers of evil were being challenged.

Question 5

Scripture (Matthew 12:29–30): “Or how can one enter into the house of the strong man and plunder his goods, unless he first bind the strong man? Then he will plunder his house. ‘He who is not with me is against me, and he who doesn’t gather with me, scatters.’”

Question: What do “bind the strong man” and “He who is not with me is against me” teach?

Answer: The strong man represents Satan; Jesus sets free those under Satan’s bondage, showing He has authority over Satan. Faced with Jesus and the work of God’s Kingdom, one must respond seriously — one cannot oppose Him while claiming to support Him.

Question 6

Scripture (Matthew 12:31–32): “Therefore I tell you, every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men, but the blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven men. Whoever speaks a word against the Son of Man, it will be forgiven him; but whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him, either in this age, or in that which is to come.”

Question: Why did Jesus warn about “blasphemy against the Holy Spirit”?

Answer: Because the Pharisees maliciously attributed the work the Holy Spirit displayed through Jesus to the prince of demons. This warns us not to stubbornly reject the Holy Spirit’s witness to Christ.

Question 7

Scripture (Matthew 12:31–32): “Therefore I tell you, every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men, but the blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven men. Whoever speaks a word against the Son of Man, it will be forgiven him; but whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him, either in this age, or in that which is to come.”

Question: How should “blasphemy against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven” be understood?

Answer: This does not refer to an accidental misspoken word, but to a stubborn, malicious rejection of the Spirit’s witness, in light of what has been seen. The warning in Scripture is extremely solemn, yet it should not be used to conclude that a person who is willing to repent and seek the Lord has no possibility of forgiveness.

Question 8

Scripture (Matthew 12:33–35): “Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree corrupt and its fruit corrupt; for the tree is known by its fruit. You offspring of vipers, how can you, being evil, speak good things? For out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks. The good man out of his good treasure brings out good things, and the evil man out of his evil treasure brings out evil things.”

Question: Why did Jesus use the tree and its fruit as an illustration?

Answer: Fruit reveals the nature of the tree; likewise, a person’s words and deeds reveal the good or evil stored up in the heart. “For out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks.”

Question 9

Scripture (Matthew 12:36): “I tell you that every idle word that men speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment.”

Question: Why must even “idle words” be accounted for on the day of judgment?

Answer: Because words that seem casual, useless, or irresponsible can still reflect a person’s heart and may hurt others. God cares about how we use our words.

Question 10

Scripture (Matthew 12:37): “For by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.”

Question: Does “by your words you will be justified… condemned” mean we are saved by speaking good words?

Answer: No. Jesus stresses that words are evidence of the condition of the heart; He does not teach that people earn salvation by good speech. True faith will gradually shape a person’s words and life.

Passage Summary

Jesus heals a man possessed by a demon, blind and mute. The crowds are amazed and take Him for the Messiah who was to come, but the Pharisees accuse Him of casting out demons by the prince of demons (vv. 22–24). Jesus shows that Satan cannot be divided against himself, and declares that by the Spirit of God He casts out demons — the Kingdom of God has come (vv. 25–30). He solemnly warns against blasphemy against the Holy Spirit (vv. 31–32), and uses the tree and its fruit to show that words reveal the heart, and that on the day of judgment people will give account for their words (vv. 33–37).

Expository Theme

Scripture (Matthew 12:25–32): “Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation, and every city or house divided against itself will not stand. If Satan casts out Satan, he is divided against himself. How then will his kingdom stand? If I by Beelzebul cast out demons, by whom do your children cast them out? Therefore they will be your judges. But if I by the Spirit of God cast out demons, then God’s Kingdom has come upon you. Or how can one enter into the house of the strong man and plunder his goods, unless he first bind the strong man? Then he will plunder his house. ‘He who is not with me is against me, and he who doesn’t gather with me, scatters.’ Therefore I tell you, every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men, but the blasphemy against the Spirit will not be forgiven men. Whoever speaks a word against the Son of Man, it will be forgiven him; but whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him, either in this age, or in that which is to come.”

Question: Faced with the accusation of “casting out demons by the prince of demons,” how did Jesus respond?

Answer: Jesus first pointed out the self-contradiction of “Satan casting out Satan,” then declared that by the Spirit of God He binds the strong man and the Kingdom of God has come, warned that blasphemy against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven, and taught that words reveal the heart, and that on the day of judgment people will give account for their words.

Application Principle

Scripture (Matthew 12:33–37): “Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree corrupt and its fruit corrupt; for the tree is known by its fruit. You offspring of vipers, how can you, being evil, speak good things? For out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks. The good man out of his good treasure brings out good things, and the evil man out of his evil treasure brings out evil things. I tell you that every idle word that men speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment. For by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.”

Question: How should Christians respond to God’s truth and live out their faith in their words?

Answer: Christians should humbly know Christ, receive the Holy Spirit’s reminders through Scripture, examine the prejudice, pride, and bitterness in their hearts, and let God renew their hearts, so that their words are truthful, careful, and able to build others up.

Application Questions

Question 1

Scripture (Matthew 12:24): “But when the Pharisees heard it, they said, ‘This man does not cast out demons except by Beelzebul, the prince of the demons.’”

Why did the Pharisees refuse to acknowledge Jesus’ authority even after seeing His miracles with their own eyes? What warning does this hold for Christians today who know the Bible well and have attended church for a long time?

Question 2

Scripture (Matthew 12:28): “But if I by the Spirit of God cast out demons, then God’s Kingdom has come upon you.”

When the Bible’s teaching conflicts with my own thinking, how do I usually respond?

Question 3

Scripture (Matthew 12:24–25): “This man does not cast out demons except by Beelzebul, the prince of the demons. Knowing their thoughts, Jesus said to them, ‘Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation, and every city or house divided against itself will not stand.’”

Do I judge other people’s motives rashly because of preconceived views?

Question 4

Scripture (Matthew 12:34): “For out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks.”

Have my words lately expressed more gratitude and encouragement, or more complaining and judgment?

Question 5

Scripture (Matthew 12:36): “I tell you that every idle word that men speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment.”

Have I ever hurt someone with a careless word? How can I make it right?

Question 6

Scripture (Matthew 12:35–37): “The good man out of his good treasure brings out good things, and the evil man out of his evil treasure brings out evil things. I tell you that every idle word that men speak, they will give account of it in the day of judgment. For by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.”

What concrete step am I willing to take this week so that both my heart and my words are more pleasing to God?
